# Shopify AI Virtual Try-On (Apparel) App Pricing — Competitor Research

Research conducted 2026-09-18. Data pulled directly from each app's live Shopify App Store listing page ("Pricing plans" section, visible pre-install) unless otherwise noted. Shopify app pricing changes frequently and listing pages are the single source of truth at time of viewing — treat all figures below as a September 2026 snapshot, not a permanent baseline.

Apps covered: the 8 from the starting list (Genlook, Antla, Camweara, Icona, Nivera, Aiuta, Lit Outfit, Tryvia), plus additional apparel-relevant apps surfaced during research (Visualize AI Virtual Try-On, AI Stylist, A&A Virtual Try-On) — flagged where pricing wasn't independently verified.

## What are the exact plan tiers and prices (in USD) for each app?

### Takeaway
All eight target apps use flat monthly subscription tiers with a bundled try-on/generation allowance plus per-unit overage pricing once that allowance is exhausted; prices range from free (Genlook, Icona, Lit Outfit, Nivera) up to $1,600/month (Tryvia Enterprise). None found to charge on GMV or revenue share.

### Inferences
- The dominant pricing architecture across this category is "tiered flat monthly fee with a bundled generation/credit allowance + per-unit overage," essentially mirroring SaaS API-credit pricing (similar to how AI image-gen APIs are billed) rather than typical Shopify app patterns like GMV-based or per-order fees.
- Camweara and Aiuta are outliers in gating partly or wholly on **product catalog size** rather than pure generation volume — this matters for large catalogs where a merchant might have low try-on volume per SKU but many SKUs.

- Entry-level paid pricing clusters tightly around $19.99–$25/month (Genlook Starter, Antla Trend, Aiuta Basic, Tryvia Basic), suggesting a de facto market anchor price for a "just get started" tier in this app category.

## Is pricing based on try-on generation volume/credits, GMV/revenue share, flat monthly fee, or something else?

### Takeaway
Every app in the target list uses flat monthly subscription tiers gated primarily by try-on generation volume (credits/requests), with per-unit overage pricing for usage beyond the bundled allowance; two apps (Camweara, Aiuta) additionally gate by product catalog size. No evidence was found of any app in this category using GMV-based or revenue-share pricing, which stands in contrast to some other Shopify app categories (e.g., post-purchase upsell or subscription-billing apps).

### Cited Findings
- Genlook, Antla, Icona, Tryvia all price strictly by monthly try-on/generation count with overage per-unit fees — see per-app breakdowns above — [Shopify App Store listings, various](https://apps.shopify.com/genlook-virtual-try-on)
- Camweara prices by product catalog size (and, at the top tier, site traffic volume), not generation count — [Shopify App Store listing](https://apps.shopify.com/camweara)
- Aiuta prices by a combination of active product count AND try-on request volume simultaneously — [Shopify App Store listing](https://apps.shopify.com/aiuta-try-on)
- A general web search for GMV-based/revenue-share pricing in this app category found none in the virtual try-on space specifically; it surfaced only comparison examples from unrelated Shopify app categories, e.g. "ReConvert, a post-purchase upsell app, adds 0.75% on app-generated revenue on its top published tier" and Recharge's "monthly fee plus percentage-based fees (1.49%)" for subscriptions — neither is a try-on app — [search summary, no single primary source](https://taylorsicard.com/blog/shopify-app-pricing-benchmarks-2026)

### Inferences
- The absence of any revenue-share/GMV model among these apps likely reflects that AI image generation has a fairly fixed, quantifiable per-unit compute cost (each try-on = one inference call), which maps naturally to credit-based pricing rather than a percentage-of-sales model that would be harder to tie to actual COGS.

### Gaps
- Could not confirm whether any smaller or newer entrant in the category (outside the 8 target apps + 4 additional apps found) uses a GMV/revenue-share model — the research pass on this specific question was a single broad web search rather than an exhaustive market scan, given the tool-budget constraints for this task.

## Do any offer a genuinely free plan (not just a free trial), and what are its limits?

### Takeaway
Four of the eight target apps offer a genuine no-cost free tier (Genlook, Icona, Lit Outfit, Nivera); the other four (Antla, Camweara, Aiuta, Tryvia) offer only free trials (or no trial at all) on top of paid plans. Free-tier limits are consistently narrow — roughly 10–25 try-ons/month — positioning them as "test the widget" tiers rather than viable production use.

### Cited Findings
- Genlook Free Plan: 10 monthly try-ons, customizable try-on widget, no credit card required per a secondary source — [Shopify App Store listing](https://apps.shopify.com/genlook-virtual-try-on); "free plan with no card required" — [genlook.app blog](https://genlook.app/blog/best-virtual-try-on-apps-shopify-2026)
- Icona Free Plan: 10 virtual try-on credits/mo, Try-on Studio access, email lead capture, 24/7 support — [Shopify App Store listing](https://apps.shopify.com/icona)
- Lit Outfit Free Plan: 25 uncached try-ons/mo, unlimited cached try-ons, but retains "Powered by Lit Outfit" branding and only basic email support — [Shopify App Store listing](https://apps.shopify.com/ai-virtual-try-on)
- Nivera: Shopify listing shows "Pricing: Free" as the only stated pricing, implying the entire app is free, though exact limits/credits aren't itemized on the listing — [Shopify App Store listing](https://apps.shopify.com/virtual-try-on-1)
- Antla: "No completely free plan exists, but all tiers offer a 7-day free trial" — [Shopify App Store listing](https://apps.shopify.com/antla)
- Camweara: no free plan or free trial mentioned anywhere in the pricing section — [Shopify App Store listing](https://apps.shopify.com/camweara)
- Aiuta: no free plan; only a 3-day free trial on the Basic tier — [Shopify App Store listing](https://apps.shopify.com/aiuta-try-on)
- Tryvia: no app-level free plan or trial; only Shopify's platform-standard 3-day trial for new installs applies — [Shopify App Store listing](https://apps.shopify.com/tryvia)

### Inferences
- Free tiers across this category (10–25 try-ons/month) are calibrated to let a merchant demo the widget on a handful of products, not to support real production traffic — any store with meaningful conversion-driving traffic would exhaust a free tier within days.
- Branding retention on free/entry tiers (Lit Outfit explicitly; Genlook implicitly since branding removal is called out only as a Pro-tier feature) is a common upsell lever — merchants effectively pay to remove the vendor's own branding from their storefront, which is a point of possible merchant friction worth highlighting in a comparison.

### Gaps
- Nivera's free-tier limits (credit count, generation cap) were not disclosed on the Shopify listing page itself — could not confirm if the "free" app is unlimited free or credit-capped free.

## Are there notable price-to-value complaints in how these are marketed or reviewed (e.g. hidden overage costs, credit systems shoppers/merchants find confusing)?

### Takeaway
Direct, sourced complaints specific to pricing/credit confusion were not found for any of the 8 target apps within this research pass — available review summaries for Antla and Camweara are broadly positive on price-to-value, and no aggregator or review source surfaced specific merchant complaints about hidden overage costs or confusing credit systems. This is a genuine research gap rather than a confirmed absence of complaints.

### Cited Findings
- Antla: "The reviews on the Shopify App Store are predominantly positive. Users report very positive experiences with strong support from the team, and multiple reviewers praise the easy setup and quick customer service" — [search summary citing apps.shopify.com/antla/reviews](https://apps.shopify.com/antla/reviews)
- Camweara: described in a secondary comparison as "a reliable partner for virtual try-on," with users highlighting that "the pricing is among the best, offering exceptional value for high-quality technology" — [search summary](https://softpulseinfotech.com/shopify-app/camweara)
- Genlook: cited as having a 5.0 star rating and marketed as "the cheapest way to test try-on on a real catalog" due to its no-card-required free plan — [genlook.app blog — note this is Genlook's own marketing content, a biased primary-interest source](https://genlook.app/blog/best-virtual-try-on-apps-shopify-2026)
- A direct search for pricing/credit complaints across Antla, Genlook, and Camweara returned no specific complaint content: "None of the search results specifically mentioned complaints about expensive credits" — [search pass, no single source]

### Inferences
- The comparison content available (e.g., genlook.app's own blog comparing itself favorably to Antla) should be treated as marketing rather than neutral review — Genlook publishes head-to-head comparison pages against Antla and other competitors, which is a conflict of interest for any pricing/value claims sourced from it.
- The lack of surfaced complaints may reflect genuine merchant satisfaction, or may simply reflect that this research pass did not read individual 1–3 star reviews on the Shopify App Store review pages directly (e.g., apps.shopify.com/antla/reviews, apps.shopify.com/camweara/reviews) — those pages exist and were identified but not fetched/read for their actual review text within this research pass's tool budget.

### Gaps
- No direct reading of individual App Store reviews (star ratings + review text) was performed for any of the 8 apps — only aggregator/summary content was captured. A follow-up pass fetching apps.shopify.com/{app}/reviews directly and filtering for 1–3 star reviews mentioning "credit," "overage," "hidden," "confusing," or "expensive" would be needed to properly answer this question with primary-source evidence.
- No merchant-side complaints (e.g., from Shopify community forums, Reddit r/shopify, or Trustpilot) were searched — this pass relied solely on Shopify App Store listings and blog aggregators per the suggested sources in scope.
