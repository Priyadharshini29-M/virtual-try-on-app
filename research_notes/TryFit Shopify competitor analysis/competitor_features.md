# Shopify AI Virtual Try-On Apps — Feature Comparison (Lead Capture, Analytics, Content Tools, Widget Customization)

Scope: Genlook, Antla, Camweara, Icona, Nivera, Aiuta, Lit Outfit, Tryvia (starting list), plus Visualize AI Virtual Try-On, AI Stylist, A&A Virtual Try-On, PictoFiT (flagged as possibly relevant). All findings are drawn from each app's Shopify App Store listing page (primary source) unless otherwise noted. Several listings are thin — absence of a feature mention is called out explicitly rather than assumed to mean the feature doesn't exist.

## Which apps have any lead-capture/email-gate mechanic at all, and how is it described?

### Takeaway
Three of the twelve apps explicitly advertise a lead-capture/email-collection feature (Genlook, Antla, Icona); none of the App Store listings describe a hard "unlock to see result" gate — where language exists, capture is framed as happening "during" or as part of the try-on flow rather than as a precondition to viewing the result. The remaining nine apps (Camweara, Nivera, Lit Outfit, Tryvia, Aiuta, Visualize AI, AI Stylist, A&A Virtual Try-On, PictoFiT) have no lead-capture mechanic mentioned anywhere in their listings.

### Cited Findings
- Genlook's listing states the widget provides "lead capture allows collecting customer emails during the try-on" and separately, under paid-tier features, "Lead capture: collect shopper emails during the try-on flow" / "Customers Email Collection" (present from the Starter tier, $19.99+/mo) — [Genlook - Shopify App Store](https://apps.shopify.com/genlook-virtual-try-on)
- Antla's app description promotes "real-time customer insights" and a review highlight quoted on the listing reads "Get emails from customers as they try outfits on"; exact timing (before vs. after the try-on image is shown) is not stated — [Antla - Shopify App Store](https://apps.shopify.com/antla)
- Icona's listing advertises "built-in email lead capture" bundled with "popups, discounts, and email collection," framed as helping with "marketing retargeting and increases sales" — implying post-try-on/follow-up capture rather than a pre-result gate, but this is not explicit — [Icona - Shopify App Store](https://apps.shopify.com/icona)
- No mention of email/phone capture appears anywhere on the Camweara, Nivera, Lit Outfit, Tryvia, Aiuta, Visualize AI, AI Stylist, A&A Virtual Try-On, or PictoFiT App Store listings — [Camweara](https://apps.shopify.com/camweara); [Nivera](https://apps.shopify.com/virtual-try-on-1); [Lit Outfit](https://apps.shopify.com/ai-virtual-try-on); [Tryvia](https://apps.shopify.com/tryvia); [Aiuta](https://apps.shopify.com/aiuta-try-on); [Visualize AI](https://apps.shopify.com/visualize-ai); [AI Stylist](https://apps.shopify.com/ai-stylist); [A&A Virtual Try-On](https://apps.shopify.com/aa-virtual-tryon); [PictoFiT](https://apps.shopify.com/pictofit-1)
- Aiuta's listing (data-access disclosure section) notes the app can access "Name, email address, phone number, physical address" as part of standard Shopify store-owner data permissions — this is a generic Shopify data-access disclosure, not a marketed lead-capture feature — [Aiuta - Shopify App Store](https://apps.shopify.com/aiuta-try-on)

### Inferences
- None of the three apps with lead capture (Genlook, Antla, Icona) describe a strict "gate" that blocks the shopper from seeing the try-on image until they submit an email — the language ("during the try-on," "as they try outfits on") suggests capture is woven into the flow (e.g., a soft prompt or a step before/alongside result delivery) rather than a hard paywall on the image itself, but the App Store copy is marketing-level and doesn't specify UX mechanics precisely enough to confirm this either way.
- Lead capture appears to correlate with the more "growth/marketing" positioned apps (Genlook, Antla, Icona all also emphasize retargeting, discounts, or social sharing) versus the more narrowly technical/return-reduction positioned apps (Camweara, PictoFiT, A&A) which do not mention it.

### Gaps
- No app's listing specifies whether email capture is mandatory or optional/skippable for the shopper.
- No app documents exactly when in the flow (pre-upload, pre-result, post-result) the email/phone field appears — this would require testing the live widget or finding a merchant walkthrough/help-doc, which was outside the App Store listing sources reviewed.
- Could not locate a Shopify listing for Aiuta describing shopper-side lead capture in the widget itself (only generic data-access permissions were found).

## Which apps have a real analytics dashboard (not just basic install-level usage stats), and how detailed is it?

### Takeaway
Genlook and Nivera advertise the most developed analytics dashboards, explicitly naming funnel and revenue/conversion metrics; Antla, Icona, Camweara, and A&A Virtual Try-On mention some form of analytics or "usage stats" but with vague, unquantified scope; Lit Outfit, Tryvia, Aiuta, Visualize AI, AI Stylist, and PictoFiT show no analytics-dashboard language at all in their listings (Tryvia mentions only generic "usage and insights").

### Cited Findings
- Genlook's listing states the widget gives merchants an "Analytics Dashboard" (Starter tier) that tracks "try-on funnel and try-on-attributed sales in one dashboard," with "Advanced Analytics" called out as an upgraded feature on Growth/Pro tiers, and separately "Track opens, try-ons and try-on-attributed sales" — no device breakdown or per-product breakdown is explicitly named — [Genlook - Shopify App Store](https://apps.shopify.com/genlook-virtual-try-on)
- Nivera's App Store subtitle itself is "AI-powered virtual try-on with analytics dashboard," and the listing describes an "in-house dashboard [that] lets you track conversions and sales made exclusively through virtual try-on," with claimed outcomes of "boost conversions by 35%, reduce returns by 25%" — [Nivera - Shopify App Store](https://apps.shopify.com/virtual-try-on-1)
- Antla's listing promotes "Real-time analytics: Track your top-performing styles, try-ons, & shares" and separately claims (via third-party/marketing copy, not the App Store listing itself) "customers see a 35% increase in conversions" — the analytics claim is about styles/try-ons/shares, not a documented conversion-funnel dashboard — [Antla - Shopify App Store](https://apps.shopify.com/antla)
- Icona's listing references "built in analytics for the virtual try on sessions & conversions" shown in a promotional screenshot, but no further metric detail (funnel stages, device split, per-product) is given in the page text — [Icona - Shopify App Store](https://apps.shopify.com/icona)
- Camweara's listing lists "Analytics, Product Status Finder" as a Lite-plan feature bullet with no elaboration on what the analytics cover — [Camweara - Shopify App Store](https://apps.shopify.com/camweara)
- A&A Virtual Try-On's listing states merchants can "Enable try-on per product with usage stats and product insights" — phrased as per-product level data but with no further metric breakdown given — [A&A Virtual Try-On - Shopify App Store](https://apps.shopify.com/aa-virtual-tryon)
- Tryvia's listing only says it "allows users to track usage and insights easily," with no dashboard, funnel, or conversion language — [Tryvia - Shopify App Store](https://apps.shopify.com/tryvia)
- Lit Outfit, Aiuta, Visualize AI, AI Stylist, and PictoFiT listings contain no analytics/dashboard language at all — [Lit Outfit](https://apps.shopify.com/ai-virtual-try-on); [Aiuta](https://apps.shopify.com/aiuta-try-on); [Visualize AI](https://apps.shopify.com/visualize-ai); [AI Stylist](https://apps.shopify.com/ai-stylist); [PictoFiT](https://apps.shopify.com/pictofit-1)

### Inferences
- Only Genlook and Nivera explicitly name "funnel" or "conversion/revenue attribution" as a dashboard concept — these two are the closest matches to a genuine try-on-to-purchase funnel analytics product among the twelve. Both are still described only at the level of marketing bullet points, not with screenshots of specific metric fields, so the actual depth (e.g., whether device/browser breakdown or per-product performance tables exist) cannot be confirmed from the listing alone.
- Apps mentioning only "usage stats," "insights," or a one-line "analytics" bullet (Antla, Icona, Camweara, A&A, Tryvia) likely offer basic session/count-level reporting (a step above bare install stats) rather than a full marketing funnel dashboard, but this is an inference from vague copy, not a confirmed fact.

### Gaps
- No listing (including Genlook's and Nivera's) explicitly confirms device-type breakdown (mobile vs. desktop) as an analytics dimension.
- No listing provides a screenshot description detailed enough to confirm true per-SKU/per-product performance tables versus aggregate site-wide numbers, except A&A's "per product" phrasing which is not elaborated further.
- Could not verify actual dashboard depth beyond App Store marketing copy — would require a merchant trial account or product screenshots/demo video review, which was out of scope for this pass.

## Which apps offer a merchant-side bulk content generation tool distinct from live shopper try-on?

### Takeaway
Visualize AI Virtual Try-On is the clearest, most detailed example — its App Store subtitle itself is "Virtual Try-On & AI Photo Studio Generate Consistent Images," with a full merchant-facing "AI Photo Studio" feature set (environments, poses, saved/reusable models, credits). Genlook and Icona also advertise a distinct merchant-side studio/content feature ("AI Studio for merchants," "Try-on Studio") but with far less detail than Visualize AI. The remaining eight apps (Antla, Camweara, Nivera, Lit Outfit, Tryvia, Aiuta, AI Stylist, A&A, PictoFiT) show no evidence of a bulk/studio content-generation tool separate from the live customer try-on widget.

### Cited Findings
- Visualize AI's listing describes: "AI Studio Environment generation for studio, indoor, outdoor, lifestyle"; "AI Photo Studio generated standing, walking, sitting, turned poses with genders"; "Saved model management to reuse consistent branded models across product pages"; and credit allotments tied to this studio feature ("250 AI Photo Studio Credits" on Basic, "1000" on Standard, "5000" on Enterprise). A merchant review quoted on the page states the tool is useful for "generating consistent content that I used throughout our store" — [Visualize AI - Shopify App Store](https://apps.shopify.com/visualize-ai)
- Genlook's listing includes a screenshot labeled "AI Studio for merchants - Create Marketing assets," described as distinct from the live shopper-facing try-on widget, though no further detail on batch size, templates, or asset types is given in the page text — [Genlook - Shopify App Store](https://apps.shopify.com/genlook-virtual-try-on)
- Icona's listing lists "Try-on Studio" as an included feature across all pricing tiers, but the page does not elaborate on bulk-generation mechanics, catalog-wide processing, or output formats — [Icona - Shopify App Store](https://apps.shopify.com/icona)
- Nivera's listing mentions merchants can "Upload custom model reference images for specific products" — this is closer to a live-try-on input customization than a distinct bulk content-generation studio; no dedicated "studio mode" or batch tool is named — [Nivera - Shopify App Store](https://apps.shopify.com/virtual-try-on-1)
- No bulk/studio content-generation tool is mentioned for Antla, Camweara, Lit Outfit, Tryvia, Aiuta, AI Stylist, A&A Virtual Try-On, or PictoFiT — [Antla](https://apps.shopify.com/antla); [Camweara](https://apps.shopify.com/camweara); [Lit Outfit](https://apps.shopify.com/ai-virtual-try-on); [Tryvia](https://apps.shopify.com/tryvia); [Aiuta](https://apps.shopify.com/aiuta-try-on); [AI Stylist](https://apps.shopify.com/ai-stylist); [A&A Virtual Try-On](https://apps.shopify.com/aa-virtual-tryon); [PictoFiT](https://apps.shopify.com/pictofit-1)
- PictoFiT's listing describes shopper-facing "pre-styled outfits," "mix & match garments," and "see outfits in different scenes & environments" — these are customer-facing styling features within the live try-on experience, not a merchant bulk-generation tool; the listing directs further questions to sales@pictofit.com and app documentation, suggesting any such capability (if it exists) is not surfaced on the App Store page — [PictoFiT - Shopify App Store](https://apps.shopify.com/pictofit-1)
- During research, additional Shopify apps not on the original list surfaced with apparent merchant-side bulk/studio positioning, notably "StyleScan AI Virtual Try-on" (App Store subtitle: "Virtually dress models in your products in minutes") and "Stylebuddy" and "Immerso" (styling-insights positioning) — these were not part of the assigned scope and were not investigated in depth, but are flagged as candidates for a follow-up pass — [StyleScan search result](https://apps.shopify.com/stylescan-studio); [Stylebuddy search result](https://apps.shopify.com/stylebuddy-virtualtryon); [Immerso search result](https://apps.shopify.com/immerso-ai-try-on-stylist)

### Inferences
- Visualize AI is positioned distinctly from the other eleven apps in this set — it markets itself first as a photo/content studio product (with virtual try-on as a secondary/bundled capability) rather than as a live try-on widget with content tools bolted on. This is the one app in scope where the merchant-facing bulk content tool is a headline feature rather than a minor bullet.
- Genlook's and Icona's "Studio" features are named but not detailed enough on the App Store listing to determine if they support true catalog-wide/bulk batch processing versus one-image-at-a-time generation for marketing use — both would need direct product trial or a dedicated feature/help page to confirm scope.

### Gaps
- Could not determine batch size limits, output image formats, or whether saved/reusable "model" assets can be applied automatically across an entire catalog for Genlook's or Icona's studio features — the App Store listings only reference the feature name and a one-line description or screenshot label.
- Could not find a Shopify App Store or product page for "Aiuta" describing any studio/bulk tool; the only accessible listing (apps.shopify.com/aiuta-try-on) describes only the live try-on button/training flow ("Add the Virtual Try-On button with one click," "Start training your products"), and the vendor's own docs.aiuta.com/shopify/ page could not be retrieved (404).
- PictoFiT's own product documentation (docs.pictofit.com) was not fetched in this pass; the App Store listing alone does not confirm or rule out a merchant-side bulk tool, since the vendor explicitly points merchants elsewhere ("contact us," linked docs) for more detail.

## What customization options are explicitly advertised for the widget (colors, button placement, branding, copy)?

### Takeaway
Widget customization is thinly documented across almost every app in this set. Where anything is mentioned, it clusters around three generic categories — brand-color matching, white-label/branding removal (usually gated to a higher paid tier), and multi-language/responsive support — rather than granular controls like button placement or freeform copy editing, which no app's listing explicitly describes.

### Cited Findings
- Genlook advertises a "Customizable try-on widget" with control over "your brand colors," support for "14+ storefront languages," a "one theme block" no-code install via Shopify Theme Editor, and a Pro-tier ($99/mo) feature to "Remove genlook branding" (implying default Genlook branding is shown on lower tiers) — [Genlook - Shopify App Store](https://apps.shopify.com/genlook-virtual-try-on)
- Antla's listing states "Full Sharing Customization" and "Full customization & embed capabilities" as pricing-tier features, plus "Fully responsive design: Across desktop, tablet, & mobile" — specific color/button/copy controls are not itemized — [Antla - Shopify App Store](https://apps.shopify.com/antla)
- Icona's listing lists "custom branding" and "mobile responsive" as features, alongside marketing language emphasizing "one-click setup," which suggests customization is simplified/limited rather than granular — [Icona - Shopify App Store](https://apps.shopify.com/icona)
- Tryvia's listing notes merchants "add the Tryvia app block to product pages via theme customization" (i.e., placement via the standard Shopify theme editor block system) and lists "Custom branding" and "Mobile responsive" as category features, with no color/copy specifics — [Tryvia - Shopify App Store](https://apps.shopify.com/tryvia)
- Lit Outfit's listing notes a white-label option — "No branding (white-label experience)" — available on its Boutique and Runway (higher) pricing plans, and that "the outfit creator is fully responsive and works on any device" — no color or button-placement controls are described — [Lit Outfit - Shopify App Store](https://apps.shopify.com/ai-virtual-try-on)
- Nivera, Camweara, Aiuta, Visualize AI, AI Stylist, A&A Virtual Try-On, and PictoFiT listings contain no specific widget color, button-placement, branding, or copy-customization language; Visualize AI and AI Stylist each mention only generic "Custom branding" / "Mobile responsive" tags with no elaboration — [Nivera](https://apps.shopify.com/virtual-try-on-1); [Camweara](https://apps.shopify.com/camweara); [Aiuta](https://apps.shopify.com/aiuta-try-on); [Visualize AI](https://apps.shopify.com/visualize-ai); [AI Stylist](https://apps.shopify.com/ai-stylist); [A&A Virtual Try-On](https://apps.shopify.com/aa-virtual-tryon); [PictoFiT](https://apps.shopify.com/pictofit-1)
- AI Stylist's listing additionally shows screenshot labels for a "Widget Setup" admin section with language selection, and a "Widget Size Advice Form" / "Widget Fit Description," suggesting some modular/configurable widget components exist, though the page gives no detail on what is actually editable within them — [AI Stylist - Shopify App Store](https://apps.shopify.com/ai-stylist)

### Inferences
- "Custom branding" is the most consistently repeated widget-customization phrase across listings (Genlook, Antla, Icona, Tryvia, Visualize AI), but it functions more as a checkbox feature tag in the App Store's standardized feature-category system than as a described capability — none of the pages explain whether "branding" means logo insertion, color theming, or just visual consistency with the merchant's site.
- White-labeling (removing the vendor's own branding from the widget) is the one customization dimension explicitly tied to a specific pricing tier in two cases (Genlook Pro tier; Lit Outfit Boutique/Runway tiers), suggesting this is treated industry-wide as a premium upsell rather than a default option.
- No app in this set advertises button-placement control, custom copy/microcopy editing, or a visual theme-color picker as a named, described feature — these controls may exist in-product (behind the "customizable widget" or "full customization" language) but are not called out explicitly enough on any App Store listing to confirm.

### Gaps
- No listing provides screenshots or descriptions detailed enough to confirm whether color customization is a color picker (any brand color) versus a small set of preset themes.
- Button placement (e.g., near "Add to Cart," as a floating button, inline in product images) is not addressed by any listing in this set.
- Copy/microcopy editing (custom call-to-action text, custom modal headlines) is not addressed by any listing in this set.
- Camweara's listing is particularly thin on merchant-configuration details generally — it emphasizes customer-facing AR/face-tracking capabilities almost exclusively — so it's unclear whether this reflects an actual lack of admin customization options or simply a listing that undersells configuration relative to competitors.
